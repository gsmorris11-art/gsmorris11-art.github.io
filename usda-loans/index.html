What a USDA loan gives you

USDA guarantees the loan, so approved lenders can offer terms that are hard to match with a low-down-payment conventional or FHA loan.

0% down

No down payment

Finance 100% of the purchase price. Savings can stay in the bank for moving costs, repairs and reserves.

0.35% a year

Low monthly fee

USDA charges an annual fee of 0.35% of the loan balance, paid monthly. That is lower than FHA mortgage insurance on a typical loan.

1% once

One-time guarantee fee

A 1% upfront fee that can be rolled into the loan, so it does not come out of pocket at closing.

30-year fixed

A payment that does not change

USDA guaranteed loans are 30-year fixed-rate mortgages. Principal and interest stay the same for the life of the loan.

No price cap

No set maximum loan amount

USDA does not publish a loan limit for the guaranteed program. What you can borrow depends on your income, debts and the appraisal.

Not first-time only

Open to repeat buyers

You do not have to be a first-time buyer. The home has to be your primary residence.

Fees shown are USDA's published rates for federal fiscal year 2027 (October 2026 through September 2027). USDA can change them each fiscal year.

The two USDA tests

Both have to pass. Checking them takes a few minutes and should happen before you fall for a house.

1. The address

The home must be in a USDA-eligible rural area. Many addresses in Culpeper, Madison, Orange and Rappahannock counties qualify, and eligibility is checked address by address on the USDA map.

2. Household income

Total household income must be under the USDA limit for the county and household size. Income from every adult living in the home counts, even if they are not on the loan.

Credit and debts

Beyond the two tests, the lender reviews credit history and monthly debts the same way it would for any mortgage.

Primary residence

USDA loans are for the home you live in. Second homes, rentals and income-producing farms are not eligible.

Check an address or your income at eligibility.sc.egov.usda.gov, the official USDA eligibility site.

USDA loans on Culpeper-area properties

USDA expects a home that is safe, sound and modest for the area. Rural details are routine when they are handled early.

Wells

Private well water has to be tested and meet health standards. Order the test as soon as you are under contract.

Septic systems

The system has to work properly. A failing system must be repaired before closing.

Acreage

Homes on acreage can qualify when the land is typical for the area and is not used to produce income.

Private roads

The home needs legal, all-weather access. A recorded road maintenance agreement keeps a private-road purchase on track.

How to buy with a USDA loan in Culpeper

USDA adds one step that other loans do not have, so build it into your contract dates.

  1. Check income and get pre-approved. Your loan officer totals household income against the county limit and reviews credit, income and assets.
  2. Shop inside the eligible area. Run each address through the USDA map before you schedule a showing.
  3. Write the offer. Sellers can contribute toward your closing costs, which helps when you are putting nothing down.
  4. Inspections and appraisal. Order the well test and septic inspection in the first week.
  5. Lender approval, then USDA review. After the lender approves the file, USDA issues its own commitment. Allow extra days for this step.
  6. Close. Plan on 30 to 45 days from contract.

USDA loan questions from Culpeper buyers

Is Culpeper eligible for USDA home loans?

Many addresses in Culpeper County and the surrounding counties fall inside USDA-eligible areas, but eligibility is decided address by address. Check any property on the USDA eligibility map, or send the address to your loan officer before you write an offer.

What is the income limit for a USDA loan in Culpeper County?

USDA sets a household income limit for each county and household size, generally 115% of the area median income, and updates it periodically. The limit counts income from every adult who will live in the home, not just the people on the loan. Look up the current figure for your county on the USDA income eligibility site.

Do I need a down payment for a USDA loan?

No. Eligible buyers can finance 100% of the purchase price, and the one-time guarantee fee can be added to the loan. You will still have closing costs, which can be paid with your own funds, gift funds or a seller credit.

What credit score do I need for a USDA loan?

USDA does not publish a hard minimum, but many lenders look for a score around 640 for a streamlined approval. Buyers below that can sometimes qualify with a manual review of rent history, income stability and savings.

Can I use a USDA loan on a home with a well and septic system?

Yes. The well water has to pass a test and the septic system has to work properly. Order both early in the contract period so results do not delay closing.

Is a USDA loan better than an FHA loan?

For buyers who qualify for both, USDA usually costs less each month because its annual fee is lower than FHA mortgage insurance and it needs no down payment. FHA has no income cap or location limit, so it fits buyers and homes that USDA does not.
