Low and no-down-payment options for first-time buyers

Most buyers qualify for more than one of these. Comparing them on the same house shows which has the lowest payment and the least cash to close.

0% down

USDA loans

No down payment for homes in eligible rural areas, with a household income limit. A strong fit for much of the land around Culpeper.

USDA loan guide

0% down

VA loans

For eligible veterans, service members and some surviving spouses. No down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance.

VA loan guide

3.5% down

FHA loans

Flexible on credit and on past credit problems. The down payment can come from a gift. Mortgage insurance is part of the payment.

FHA loan guide

3% down

Conventional loans

First-time buyer programs start at 3% down. Mortgage insurance drops off once you build enough equity, which FHA does not allow on most loans.

Grant

Virginia Housing grant

A Down Payment Assistance Grant of 2% to 2.5% of the price for qualified first-time buyers. It is a grant, so it is never repaid.

0% down

Virginia Housing Plus Second

A second 30-year fixed mortgage that covers the down payment on an eligible Virginia Housing loan. Has higher income limits than the grant.

Virginia Housing programs have income and price limits that vary by county and change over time. Current limits are at virginiahousing.com.

The cash you need, beyond the down payment

A no-down-payment loan is not a no-cash purchase. Knowing these four items up front prevents surprises.

Earnest money

A deposit that goes with your offer and is credited back to you at closing. The amount is negotiated in the contract.

Inspections

Home inspection, and on rural homes a well water test and septic inspection. Paid when the work is done, before closing.

Closing costs

Lender fees, title, recording and the first year of homeowners insurance, plus deposits for taxes and insurance. A seller credit can cover part of them.

A cushion

Money left over after closing for the first repair, the move and the things every new house needs.

How to buy your first home, in order

The first three steps cost nothing and can be done months before you are ready to buy.

  1. Check your credit and budget. Decide on a monthly payment you are comfortable with, not just the most a lender will approve.
  2. Take the homebuyer class. Virginia Housing's class is free and online. It is required for Virginia Housing first-time buyer loans, and the certificate lasts two years.
  3. Get fully pre-approved. Income, assets and credit are reviewed up front, and you see each loan option priced on the same day.
  4. Find an agent and shop. A local agent knows which homes fit your loan type, including USDA-eligible addresses.
  5. Make an offer. Your agent can ask the seller to help with closing costs.
  6. Inspect, appraise, close. Most purchases close 30 to 45 days after the contract is signed.

First-time buyer questions from Culpeper

How much money do I need to buy my first home in Culpeper?

It depends on the loan. VA and USDA loans need no down payment, FHA needs 3.5% and some conventional loans need 3%. On top of the down payment, plan for closing costs, an earnest money deposit and inspections. Seller credits, gift funds and Virginia Housing assistance can cover part of that.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer in Virginia?

For Virginia Housing programs, you are a first-time buyer if you have not owned and lived in a primary residence in the last three years. Someone who owned a home years ago can qualify again.

Is there down payment assistance for first-time buyers in Virginia?

Yes. Virginia Housing offers a Down Payment Assistance Grant of 2% to 2.5% of the purchase price that is never repaid, and a Plus Second Mortgage that covers the down payment with a second 30-year fixed loan. Both have income limits and must be paired with a Virginia Housing first mortgage.

What credit score do I need to buy a house?

There is no single number. FHA allows a 3.5% down payment with a score of 580, Virginia Housing loans generally start at 620 to 640, and conventional loans price best with higher scores. Lenders can set their own minimums, so ask before you assume you do not qualify.

Do I have to take a homebuyer class?

Virginia Housing requires first-time buyers using its loans to complete a homebuyer education course. Virginia Housing's own class is free, can be taken online, and the certificate is good for two years.

Should I get pre-approved before I look at houses?

Yes. A full pre-approval tells you the price range and monthly payment you can live with, and sellers in Culpeper give more weight to offers that come with one.
