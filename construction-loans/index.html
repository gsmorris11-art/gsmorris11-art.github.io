How a construction loan works

A construction-to-permanent loan covers the build and then becomes your long-term mortgage, so you are not shopping for a second loan when the house is finished.

One closing

One loan from dirt to done

With a one-time-close loan you sign once, before the build starts. That means one set of closing costs and no need to re-qualify when the home is complete.

Draws

The builder is paid in stages

Money is released as each phase is finished and inspected: foundation, framing, mechanicals, finishes. You are never paying for work that has not been done.

Land equity

Your land can be your down payment

If you own your land outright or have equity in it, that value can count toward the down payment. Some owners bring little or no cash to closing.

Land + home

No land yet? Finance both

The lot purchase and the build can be combined in one loan, so you are not carrying a separate land loan while you wait to start.

Budget

The whole project in one number

Site work, well, septic, driveway, permits and a contingency reserve can be built into the loan along with the house itself.

Permanent

A regular mortgage at the end

When the home passes its final inspection, the loan converts to a standard fixed-rate mortgage. You move in and start normal payments.

What you can build

The right loan depends on the kind of home. All three of these can be financed with the land as real estate.

Site-built homes

A custom or semi-custom home built on your lot by a licensed general contractor. The most flexible design and the longest build time.

Modular homes

Built in sections in a factory to the same building code as a site-built home, then set on a permanent foundation. Appraised and financed like a site-built home.

Manufactured homes

New multi-section homes placed on a permanent foundation and titled as real estate. Often the lowest cost per square foot and the fastest path to move-in.

Building on Culpeper-area land

Most building lots outside town limits have no public water or sewer. Sorting out these items early keeps the loan and the builder on schedule.

Septic and soil

The lot needs soil work and a health department permit for a septic system before a lender can close. If you are buying land, make the contract contingent on it.

Well

Drilling cost depends on depth, which nobody knows until the rig is on site. Build an allowance and a cushion into the budget.

Access and driveway

The lot needs legal, recorded access. Private and shared roads usually need a road maintenance agreement, and a new entrance may need a VDOT permit.

Zoning and permits

Each county sets its own setbacks, minimum lot sizes and rules for manufactured homes. Confirm what the lot allows before you pay for plans.

How to get from land to move-in

The first two steps cost nothing and tell you what budget to take to a builder.

  1. Get pre-approved for the full project. Income, assets and credit are reviewed up front so you know your total budget for land, site work and the home.
  2. Confirm the land is buildable. Soil evaluation, septic permit, access and zoning come before plans and deposits.
  3. Choose a builder or home dealer. The lender reviews the builder's license, insurance and track record, plus your signed contract, plans and specifications.
  4. Appraisal and closing. The appraiser values the finished home from the plans. You close once, and the builder can start.
  5. Build, inspect, draw. Each stage is inspected before the builder is paid for it.
  6. Final inspection and move-in. The loan converts to your permanent mortgage.

Construction loan questions from Culpeper-area buyers

How does a construction loan work in Virginia?

A construction-to-permanent loan pays your builder in stages, called draws, as the work is completed and inspected. When the home is finished, the loan becomes a regular long-term mortgage. With a one-time-close loan you sign one set of closing documents before the build starts.

How much down payment do I need to build a house?

It depends on the loan type and your eligibility. Some government-backed construction programs allow little or no down payment for eligible borrowers, while conventional construction loans usually require more. Equity in land you already own can count toward the down payment.

Can I use land I already own as my down payment?

Often, yes. If you own your land outright or have built up equity in it, that value can be applied toward the down payment, and in some cases it covers the whole requirement so you bring little or no cash to closing.

Can I finance a modular or manufactured home on my own land?

Yes. Modular homes are financed much like site-built homes. New manufactured homes can be financed with the land when they are placed on a permanent foundation and titled as real estate, and they must meet the requirements of the loan program.

Do I make payments while the house is being built?

That depends on the loan. Many construction loans charge interest only on the money drawn so far during the build, and some programs defer payments until the home is complete. Your loan officer will show you both the construction-phase and the permanent payment before you commit.

How long does it take to build a home in the Culpeper area?

Site-built homes commonly take six to twelve months from breaking ground, depending on the builder, weather and permits. Modular and manufactured homes are usually faster because most of the work happens in a factory while the site is prepared.
